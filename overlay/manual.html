Getting started

Two things need teaching once: where the table is in the camera picture, and — if you are projecting — where the table is on the projector. They are separate, and doing one does nothing for the other.

  1. Point the camera at the table. As high and central as the room allows. Fix it to something solid: if it gets knocked, everything it tells you afterwards is wrong.
  2. Print the markers. Setup → Camera → Print the markers, or go straight to the marker sheet. Print at 100%, cut out the four squares leaving the white border on, and tape each one flat on the cloth just inside the corner it names, outer corner on the cushion corner.
  3. Calibrate. Setup → Camera → Calibrate from markers. Leave the markers taped down — then if the camera is ever nudged it can fix itself.
  4. Check it. Open the projector page and press b. It draws where it thinks each ball is. They should land on the real balls. This is the only check that matters.
  5. Pick a game and press Start.

No markers to hand? The same tab lets you tap the four corners yourself. Use the magnifier and take your time: three pixels of sloppiness costs about 5 mm on the cloth, which is enough to misjudge a ball hanging over a pocket.

Playing

Rack up and play. The table reads each shot once the balls stop, so nothing needs pressing between shots. The scoreboard shows whose turn it is, what each player has left, and a running feed of what happened.

Fixing a wrong score

It will misread something eventually, especially before the detector is tuned to your cloth and lights. Two controls, both on the scoreboard:

Undo (top bar)takes back the last shot — the score, whose turn it is, group assignments, two-shot state, even a frame it wrongly called as won. Press it again to go further back.
+ / − on a player cardsets a score by hand. Undo restores the old score; only you know the right one.

Undo rewinds the score, not the table. The balls stay where they are, so the next shot is judged from the layout in front of you.

Ball in hand

After a foul the table marks where the cue ball may go and checks where you put it. If it is touching a ball, off the table, or in the wrong half for the rules you are playing, it says so and shows the nearest legal spot — a suggestion, not an instruction.

Which area counts as legal depends on the code. UK World Rules and the American game give ball in hand anywhere; plenty of pubs still play it from baulk. It is a setting, not an assumption.

The games

8-BallAmerican rules: called pocket on the 8
English 8-BallUK rules: two shots after a foul, no called pocket
Blackballinternational UK code: one shot with a free shot after a foul
9-Balllowest ball first, pot the 9 — needs numbered balls
Cut-Throatthree players, five balls each — needs numbered balls
Killerpot on your turn or lose a life
Hotspotsroll object balls through the lit zones
Pocket Roulettethe table picks which pocket you must use
Position Drillpot, then stop the cue ball in the ring — graded in millimetres
Time Bomb30 seconds on the clock, +7 a ball; clear the table before it runs out

Time Bomb and the clock. The clock stops at the moment you strike, not when the balls stop rolling, so a long pot played with a second to spare still counts. It also holds while the balls are moving — watching a ball roll is not thinking time. Potting the cue ball ends the game; set a scratch penalty in seconds instead if that is too harsh for a pub night. With more than one player everybody gets their own clock, and whoever potted the most when the last one blows takes it.

With reds and yellows, 9-Ball and Cut-Throat are unavailable — no camera can tell one red from another, so rather than guess, they are switched off. Everything else plays normally.

Camera

Recalibrate whenever the camera moves. With the markers still taped down that is one button; without them it is four careful taps.

Average error under 5 mmgood
5–15 mmusable, but balls near a pocket may be judged wrongly
Over 15 mmdo it again

If balls go missing in dark corners, light the table more evenly before changing any setting.

If you have a Kinect, depth is the one thing shadows cannot touch — a shadow has no depth and neither does glare, so a ball is simply 51 mm of "nearer than the cloth" under any light at all. Mount it 1.8–2.2 m above the cloth, landscape, long way along the table: below about 1.6 m the depth sensor cannot see the whole cloth, and above 4 m its depth readings stop being reliable. Then Setup → Camera → Calibrate depth — it needs its own four corners, because the depth sensor is 512×424 with a different field of view and the colour calibration does not transfer. Tap the inside corners of the bright rail, save, then Use depth to find balls. Depth says where they are; colour still says which is which.

Setup → Check holds the three things the camera needs to be told, all of which take effect on the next restart:

Which ballsreds and yellows, or numbered 1–15. Picks both the playable games and the colour model.
Where the camera isoverhead, long side or end on, plus the height. Removes parallax — about 26 mm on a side mount, against a 70 mm pocket.
Ball sizeEnglish 25.4 mm radius, American 28.6. The detector reads each ball's colour from a patch this size.

Whether to use YOLO instead of colour is on the Detector tab. Try colour first: reds and yellows are two well separated hues, which is the easy case, and YOLO is a large download that finds balls by shape.

Projector

Review

Every game is recorded. Review plays them back shot by shot, animating the path each ball actually took, with the figures that tell you something you can act on:

Pot rateare you making balls
Fouls, and which kindhow you hand the table over
Cue ball movedposition play — less is usually better
Best runthe one everyone wants to know

space plays and pauses, arrow keys step a shot at a time.

Recordings survive a restart. If the computer is closed mid-frame, the scores can be handed straight back — the balls are wherever they are, but the numbers are not lost.

When it goes wrong

Discs are offset at one end of the table calibration — redo it, ideally with markers
Balls missing in the corners uneven lighting, or the camera cannot see them
A ball vanishes and scores as potted glare or a shadow edge — press Undo, then fix the lighting
Everything drifted after a knock recalibrate; leave the markers up next time
Nothing is detected at all restart with an empty table so it can learn the cloth
It says the view is obstructed something is over the table — that is working as intended
